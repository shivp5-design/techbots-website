Our Innovation Project – FLL 2025-2026 (Unearthed): Bhumibots

Modern archaeology still relies heavily on inefficient excavation methods that require extensive physical labor and large amounts of time, often with uncertain results. Archaeologists frequently dig for long periods without discovering any artifacts, which can waste valuable resources and risk damaging historical sites. To solve this problem, our team developed Bhumibots: a swarm of nanobots designed to locate underground artifacts before excavation begins. Bhumibots use advanced technologies from synthetic biology and nanotechnology, including Misthetic Sensor Fimbriae for high-precision environmental sensing, Biological Sensor Domes capable of detecting artifact-specific chemical and material signatures, Quantum Entanglement Emitters for secure data transmission, and a self-sustaining Microfluidic Etcolorfal Energy Core to enable long-term underground operation since it harvests energy from the soil. The concept of energy in the soil has already been explored by the Northwestern University Research Team as SMFCs (Soil Microbial Fuel Cells). Instead of guessing where artifacts might be located, archaeologists can deploy a swarm of Bhumibots across a site. The nanobots travel underground, scan the area, and precisely identify artifact locations, eliminating unnecessary digging and reducing site damage. This solution is innovative because nanobots have primarily been used in the medical field, such as detecting and destroying cancer cells. Our project reimagines that technology for archaeology, transforming how historical discoveries are made and making excavation faster, safer, and more accurate.

Robot Design

This season, our design was centered on efficiency and precision in every aspect of our robot’s performance. We streamlined the overall structure by removing non-essential components, which reduced mass and minimized friction, allowing for smoother, faster, and more consistent movement across the field. By optimizing our drivetrain and programming strategy, we made efficient use of our time during matches, enabling simultaneous movement and more complex, coordinated maneuvers within a single run. We also addressed our primary bottleneck-attachment transition time by implementing a bevel gear mechanism that allows us to swap mission-specific tools instantly and securely. This innovation ensures our robot remains both versatile and highly efficient throughout every match. Additionally, we designed a gravity-based attachment system that slides smoothly onto the robot and can be removed within a split second. Using gravity for alignment and locking reduces mechanical complexity while maintaining stability, giving us rapid, reliable transitions that maximize scoring opportunities. To further enhance precision, we incorporated a custom-built jig to position our robot before each run, enabling a 99.99% accuracy rate in alignment. This ensures consistent starting orientation, reduces human error, and allows our autonomous programs to execute with maximum reliability and repeatability.
